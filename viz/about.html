About NYC budget data

We publish New York City's budget as data you can search and check, in two halves. City budget: every City agency's adopted and modified budget, FY2017–FY2027, from NYC Open Data. Council discretionary funding: which organizations the Council funds, for how much, from the Council's own spreadsheets, its budget PDFs, and NYC Open Data. Every number comes from the City's own data, and we never change one by hand.

Which tool answers my question?

Your questionWhere to look
How much does my community board get?City agency budgets. Each of the 59 boards is its own agency (Manhattan 341–352, Bronx 381–392, Brooklyn 471–488, Queens 431–444, Staten Island 491–493).
How did an agency's budget change, year to year or during the year?City agency budgets: Adopted and Modified, by year.
Which groups did my Council member fund? Did this nonprofit get Council money?Council discretionary funding: the expense awards (search by name or EIN).
What did the Council change after the budget was adopted? Which capital projects did it fund?Council discretionary funding: transparency resolutions; capital and the capital book.
Was the money actually spent? Did an award become a contract?Checkbook NYC (the Comptroller). This site has budgets, not spending.
Revenue, debt, or spending over decades?The Independent Budget Office's Data Center.

The words used here are explained in the glossary below.

Glossary

Fiscal year (FY)
New York City's budget year runs July 1 to June 30 and is named for the year it ends. FY2026 is July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026. Every year on this site is a fiscal year.
Adopted budget
The budget the Mayor and Council agree in June, before the fiscal year starts.
Modified budget
The adopted budget as changed during the year. In the City budget half, "Modified" is the figure as of June, near the year's end; Checkbook NYC's year-end figure can differ slightly. The newest year's Modified is "Not yet published": the City publishes it in the release that adopts the next year's budget.
Preliminary, Executive and Adopted releases
OMB publishes each year's budget up to three times: Preliminary (January), Executive (April or May) and Adopted (June). Each restates the whole budget, so they must never be added together. The City budget half uses only the June release.
Which year a figure belongs to
NYC Open Data's Expense Budget labels each June release with the coming fiscal year, but two of the three amounts in it describe the year before. So we take a year's adopted budget from the release labeled with that year, and its modified budget from the next year's release. Until November 9, 2026, the City budget page got this wrong and showed every year one year late.
Agency
A City department or office with its own budget, such as the Department of Education. Each community board is its own agency.
Community board
One of New York City's 59 community boards. In the City's budget, each board is its own agency, with its own agency number. The numbers come from OMB's budget data on NYC Open Data (the Expense Budget's agency number): Manhattan 341–352, Bronx 381–392, Brooklyn 471–488, Queens 431–444, and Staten Island 491–493. Board n in a borough is the first number plus n−1: Brooklyn Community Board 6 is 476.
Unit of appropriation
The lines the expense budget is adopted by. An agency has one or more, often split into Personal Services and Other Than Personal Services.
Personal Services (PS) and Other Than Personal Services (OTPS)
PS is pay for the agency's staff. OTPS is everything else: supplies, contracts, rent and so on.
Intra-city
Money one City agency pays another for services it buys from it. The same dollars appear in both agencies' budgets.
Net and gross
OMB's headline "net total budget" leaves intra-city money out, so it is not counted twice. NYC Open Data rows include it, so a total added up from Open Data is gross. FY2026 adopted: $115.9 billion net, $117,791,473,676 gross as counted in Open Data, of which $1,884,163,949 is intra-city.
Expense and capital budgets
The expense budget pays for running the City each year: staff, services, supplies. The capital budget pays for things that last: buildings, parks, equipment. The City budget half is the expense budget. The Council half has both.
Council discretionary funding
Money in the budget that the Council, not the Mayor, directs to particular organizations and programs. It is a small part of the budget.
Schedule C
The list, printed with the adopted budget, of the Council's discretionary awards, with each award's initiative and category.
Initiative
In Schedule C, the program a discretionary award is funded under.
Transparency resolution
A Council resolution, after the budget is adopted, that adds, removes or changes discretionary awards.
Expense funding disclosure
The Council's spreadsheet of discretionary awards, with each grantee's EIN, amount and clearance status. It is a later list than Schedule C, with awards added during the year.
Clearance status
The status the disclosure gives each award, such as Cleared or Pending. An early snapshot (FY2009, FY2013) has many awards still Pending, so its Cleared total is not the year's outcome.
Capital designation
Capital money the Council directs to a particular project, from the Council's capital lists.
Capital book (§254 supporting detail)
The detail behind the capital changes the Council adopts under Section 254 of the City Charter: the Council's capital additions, project by project.
Budget line
A line of the City's capital budget. The Council explorer compares the Council's capital money with OMB's, budget line by budget line.
Terms and Conditions
The reports the adopted budget requires agencies to make.
EIN
An organization's federal tax ID number.
MOCS ID
The Mayor's Office of Contract Services' ID for an award, which NYC Open Data adds to matching disclosure rows (FY2009–FY2021).
OMB
The Mayor's Office of Management and Budget. It prepares the budget and publishes the agency data in mwzb-yiwb.
NYC Open Data
The City's open data portal, data.cityofnewyork.us. Each dataset has a short ID, such as mwzb-yiwb.
MCP server
A tool an AI assistant can use. BetaNYC's budget MCP server lets an assistant search the Council data and answer with the source of each row.

How the Council's PDF tables are checked

We read every row of the Council's FY2018–FY2027 budget PDFs twice, once from the PDF's text and once from a picture of the page, and tied the rows to every total the PDFs print. Where the two readings or the totals disagree, a person reviews it. We never change a number: values are shown exactly as printed.

  • 141,456 rows in 36 tables: Schedule C, the transparency resolutions, the capital book and Terms and Conditions.
  • Schedule C: 76,216 of 76,272 rows read the same both ways. The other 56 went to a person.
  • 515 of 519 printed totals tie exactly. The other 4 are totals the PDF itself gets wrong, and they are flagged, not fixed.
  • The review is still going. Every row is labeled "checked, review in progress" until a person has decided every flagged item for its year. No year is finished yet.

Figures as of the pdf-tables-2026-10-24-260d16e data release. FY2017 and earlier are not re-read yet.

The Council's spreadsheets and NYC Open Data

  • Discretionary expense designations: the Council's own expense funding disclosure spreadsheets, FY2009 and FY2011 through FY2027, one row per published designation, with clearance status and the grantee's mailing address. FY2010 is not included: the Council's archived workbook for that year is incomplete against NYC Open Data's. FY2009 and FY2013 are early snapshots, frozen while many designations were still pending.
  • Capital designations: the Council's own capital workbooks and NYC Open Data, FY2015 through FY2017 and FY2019 through FY2027. FY2018 is missing from every source found.

How we use AI

We use AI tools openly, and people stay accountable for the result. AI agents (Anthropic's Claude) wrote much of the code, directed and reviewed by BetaNYC staff. This follows our AI policy.

The numbers are not made by AI. Agency figures come from NYC Open Data and are added up by a script. Council spreadsheet values are copied cell by cell. The Council's PDF tables are read from each PDF's own text, then checked against a picture of the page with ordinary image processing, not an AI model. Every row keeps its source.

One exception: the FY2009 transparency resolutions are scanned pages with no text, so an AI model read them. That data is not on this site; it stays in the repository for research.
