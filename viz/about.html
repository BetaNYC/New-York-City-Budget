About NYC budget data

BetaNYC publishes New York City's budget as data you can search and check, in two halves. City budget: every City agency's adopted and modified budget, FY2017–FY2027, from NYC Open Data. Council discretionary funding: which organizations the Council funds, for how much, from the Council's own spreadsheets, its budget PDFs and NYC Open Data. Every number comes from the City's own data, and we never change one by hand.

Glossary

Fiscal year (FY)
New York City's budget year runs July 1 to June 30 and is named for the year it ends. FY2026 is July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026. Every year on this site is a fiscal year.
Adopted budget
The budget the Mayor and Council agree in June, before the fiscal year starts.
Modified budget
The adopted budget as changed during the year. In the City budget half, "Modified" is the figure as of June, near the year's end; Checkbook NYC's year-end figure can differ slightly. The newest year's Modified is "Not yet published": the City publishes it in the release that adopts the next year's budget.
Preliminary, Executive and Adopted releases
OMB publishes each year's budget up to three times: Preliminary (January), Executive (April or May) and Adopted (June). Each restates the whole budget, so they must never be added together. The City budget half uses only the June release.
The mwzb-yiwb year rule
In the Open Data release tagged fiscal_year = Y, financial_plan_amount is FY Y and the other two amounts are FY Y−1. So Adopted Y = financial_plan_amount from the June release tagged Y, and Modified Y = current_modified_budget_amount from the June release tagged Y+1.
Agency
A City department or office with its own budget, such as the Department of Education. Each community board is its own agency.
Community board
One of New York City's 59 community boards. Each is its own agency in the budget: Manhattan 341–352, Bronx 381–392, Brooklyn 471–488, Queens 431–444, Staten Island 491–493. Board n in a borough is the first code plus n−1: Brooklyn Community Board 6 is 476.
Unit of appropriation
The lines the expense budget is adopted by. An agency has one or more, often split into Personal Services and Other Than Personal Services.
Personal Services (PS) and Other Than Personal Services (OTPS)
PS is pay for the agency's staff. OTPS is everything else: supplies, contracts, rent and so on.
Intra-city
Money one City agency pays another for services it buys from it. The same dollars appear in both agencies' budgets.
Net and gross
OMB's headline "net total budget" leaves intra-city money out, so it is not counted twice. NYC Open Data rows include it, so a total added up from Open Data is gross. FY2026 adopted: $115.9 billion net, $117,791,473,676 gross as counted in Open Data, of which $1,884,163,949 is intra-city.
Expense and capital budgets
The expense budget pays for running the City each year: staff, services, supplies. The capital budget pays for things that last: buildings, parks, equipment. The City budget half is the expense budget. The Council half has both.
Council discretionary funding
Money in the budget that the Council, not the Mayor, directs to particular organizations and programs. It is a small part of the budget.
Schedule C
The list, printed with the adopted budget, of the Council's discretionary awards, with each award's initiative and category.
Initiative
In Schedule C, the program a discretionary award is funded under.
Transparency resolution
A Council resolution, after the budget is adopted, that adds, removes or changes discretionary awards.
Expense funding disclosure
The Council's spreadsheet of discretionary awards, with each grantee's EIN, amount and clearance status. It is a later list than Schedule C, with awards added during the year.
Clearance status
The status the disclosure gives each award, such as Cleared or Pending. An early snapshot (FY2009, FY2013) has many awards still Pending, so its Cleared total is not the year's outcome.
Capital designation
Capital money the Council directs to a particular project, from the Council's capital lists.
Capital book (§254 supporting detail)
The detail behind the capital changes the Council adopts under Section 254 of the City Charter: the Council's capital additions, project by project.
Budget line
A line of the City's capital budget. The Council explorer compares the Council's capital money with OMB's, budget line by budget line.
Terms and Conditions
The reports the adopted budget requires agencies to make.
EIN
An organization's federal tax ID number.
MOCS ID
The Mayor's Office of Contract Services' ID for an award, which NYC Open Data adds to matching disclosure rows (FY2009–FY2021).
OMB
The Mayor's Office of Management and Budget. It prepares the budget and publishes the agency data in mwzb-yiwb.
NYC Open Data
The City's open data portal, data.cityofnewyork.us. Each dataset has a short ID, such as mwzb-yiwb.
MCP server
A tool an AI assistant can use. BetaNYC's @betanyc/nyc-budget-mcp lets an assistant search the Council data and answer with the source of each row.

How we use AI

BetaNYC uses AI tools openly and with people accountable for the result. The code here was built by AI agents (Anthropic's Claude) working under the direction and review of BetaNYC staff. We tell you this because transparency is part of BetaNYC's AI policy.

The figures are not AI-generated. Agency figures are read from NYC Open Data's API and summed by a script. Council spreadsheet values are read cell by cell. PDF values are read from the PDF's own text and ruled lines, and checked against an image of the page by ordinary image processing, not a language model. Where a person confirmed that the page shows something the text layer doesn't, the row says so. Every row keeps its source.

One exception, not served: the FY2009 transparency resolutions are scans with no text, so they were read by OCR (a model). They stay in the repository as a research dataset.
